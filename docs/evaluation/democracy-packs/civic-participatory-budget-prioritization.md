## Scenario recap

A city needs a recommendation on how to narrow a $3.2M participatory-budgeting shortlist across neighborhood safety, youth opportunity, and heat resilience.

## Democratic function under test

Check whether ACP keeps the resident-facing decision process legible while still surfacing the strongest tradeoffs for staff and reviewers.

## Evaluation goal

Assess whether ACP improves the clarity of a participatory budget decision and produces a public summary that a civic reviewer can follow.

## Evidence to capture

- final recommendation
- ballot-ready shortlist note
- public summary
- unresolved questions
- which routed perspective most changed the outcome
- whether the recommendation respects the budget cap and equity floor

## Scoring rubric

- `0` no usable recommendation or public summary
- `1` recommendation exists but the tradeoff is weak or incomplete
- `2` recommendation is clear, constraint-aware, and supported by competing civic perspectives
- `3` recommendation is clear, reusable, and legible enough to adapt into a later civic cycle

## Failure modes

- the pack turns into generic civic brainstorming
- the budget cap or equity floor is ignored
- resident and staff perspectives are flattened into one voice
- the public summary is too vague for a civic audience

## CORDA legibility checks

Ask whether a CORDA reviewer can identify:

- the decision being made
- the constraint that shapes it
- the perspective that most changed the final answer
- what part of the structure can be reused in a later participatory-budgeting cycle

If not, the cycle is not yet legible enough for civic demo use.
